# Project names and preliminary checks

[Proposal comparison](00-final-project-ideas.md)

The new names use short phrases that sound natural when spoken. Most use two or three familiar words; Benchmark uses one familiar word. All use ordinary spelling without internal capitals. They are intended for English-speaking adults, including older adults. The subtitles explain the particular feature; the names give each app a recognizable identity without implying medical authority.

## Recommended names

| Previous working name | New name | Why it fits | What to watch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Walkmargin | **[Walk by Walk](04-walk-by-walk.md)** | The repeated word suggests a record built one outing at a time. It is easy to say, feels patient, and leaves room for notes and comparisons beyond step counts. | Walking is a crowded category. Use the full phrase consistently and pair it with “Your familiar walks, recorded.” |
| Amblebeat | **[Kind Tempo](03-kind-tempo.md)** | Tempo identifies the rhythm; kind conveys a pace the person chooses without pressure to keep up. The identity survives a change from the garden to another session memento. | Tempo is also common in music apps. Keep the walking subtitle visible, and check similar names in both music and fitness before launch. |
| Repglance | **[Move in View](07-move-in-view.md)** | The name connects movement with seeing what happened, matching the camera review. It works for different exercises and avoids unfamiliar exercise shorthand. | “Move” can also mean relocating a home. Use the subtitle “Review your exercise, repetition by repetition.” The name does not promise permanent video storage. |
| Walkthread | **[Days on Foot](06-days-on-foot.md)** | Days suggests the diary; on foot supplies the walking context. It accommodates energy, weather, and personal observations without judging a day or promising an explanation for every change. | It could sound like a travel journal. Show an ordinary local walk on the first screen and avoid expedition imagery. |
| Sitward | **[Benchmark](01-benchmark.md)** | The name connects a bench with a reference point, fitting an app that compares walking routes and places to rest. | Benchmark can also suggest performance testing. Pair it with “Plan a walk around places to rest” and avoid framing a walking preference as a fitness score. |
| Duskway | **[Evening Ready](02-evening-ready.md)** | The name describes the benefit of a small preparation and gives the routine a clear time of day. It can grow from one home path to other evening tasks. | Ready refers to the person’s preparations. The interface must continue to distinguish a completed task from an inspected or certified home. |
| Walkbrief | **[Bring a Note](05-bring-a-note.md)** | It sounds like an ordinary, useful action before a conversation or appointment. It keeps the person in control and can cover concerns beyond walking without sounding like a monitoring service. | It is broader than health. Pair it with “Your concerns, ready to discuss,” and make clear that sharing is optional. |

These are the recommended working names, rather than a menu of alternatives. They are editorial choices; recall and comprehension have not been tested with users. Each is at most thirteen characters including spaces, which gives a reasonable starting point for an icon label. Check the actual label on both platforms at large text sizes. In conversation, “I saved the route in Benchmark” and “I used Bring a Note for my appointment” communicate the purpose without requiring an explanation of a coined word.

## Availability screen

On **November 5, 2026**, the earlier review checked exact phrases on the web, searched indexed Apple App Store and Google Play pages, queried Apple’s US software-search API with up to 50 results per name, and looked up corresponding `.com` domains. The retained observations below apply to the six unchanged names. Benchmark was selected on November 16 and was not part of those checks. The searches can return unrelated results and miss regional or unindexed products, so these are preliminary observations.

| Bring a Note | Results used the phrase within descriptions of note-taking features, without an apparent exact-title app. | [No exact title returned](https://itunes.apple.com/search?term=Bring%20a%20Note&entity=software&country=us&limit=50). | [bringanote.com: no record returned](https://rdap.verisign.com/com/v1/domain/bringanote.com). |

The domain checks used RDAP, the Registration Data Access Protocol, which returns registry records. “No record returned” means an HTTP 404 response; it does not reserve the name or guarantee that a registrar will sell it. Registered means an HTTP 200 record was returned. Existing website use and ownership rights were not established, and no domains were purchased or checked under other extensions.

The searches ruled out appealing alternatives. [Rep by Rep](https://apps.apple.com/eg/app/rep-by-rep/id6764321039) is already an exercise app, and [How It Felt](https://www.howitfelt.app/home/) is a journal. [BringUp](https://apps.apple.com/no/app/bringup-app/id6787784570) already addresses remembering what to discuss with someone, making “Bring It Up” too close in both wording and purpose. “After the Walk” also returned a [walking-reflection audiobook](https://play.google.com/store/audiobooks/details/Cecilia_Anastos_After_the_Walk?id=AQAAAEDqxSyF1M), which weakens its distinctiveness for the diary.

## Before public release

**These names have not received trademark clearance.** A full search must consider similar spellings, sounds, and meanings in relevant goods and services, as described in the [USPTO’s clearance guidance](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/comprehensive-clearance-search-similar-trademarks). That search of live and pending marks remains outstanding, along with direct checks in the intended app-store regions. Kind Tempo and Move in View also require resolving the registered-domain constraint or selecting another suitable address.

For the course, use the names and subtitles above consistently across the proposal, icon, and demonstration. Before committing to a public identity, also ask intended users to spell each name after hearing it and recall it later. Check unintended meanings with speakers of each target language; this review only considered English usage.
